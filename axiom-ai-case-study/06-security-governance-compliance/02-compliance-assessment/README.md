# Compliance Assessment

## Overview

This section evaluates the effectiveness and alignment of Axiom AI Technologies' information security program against selected regulatory, contractual, and security framework requirements.

The assessment builds on the risk management, controls assessment, ISMS implementation, business continuity, vulnerability management, and security remediation activities completed in earlier projects.

The objective is to demonstrate how a GRC analyst can translate security and compliance requirements into measurable assessment criteria, identify gaps, evaluate risk, document evidence, and track remediation activities.

---

## Objectives

The compliance assessment is designed to:

* Evaluate compliance against selected security and governance requirements.
* Map requirements to applicable security controls.
* Assess the effectiveness and implementation status of controls.
* Identify compliance gaps and deficiencies.
* Evaluate the risk associated with identified gaps.
* Document supporting evidence and evidence gaps.
* Assign ownership for remediation activities.
* Establish target dates and remediation priorities.
* Provide management with a consolidated view of compliance posture.
* Support continual improvement of the Axiom security program.

## Assessment Methodology

The assessment follows a structured compliance evaluation process.

### 1. Requirement Identification

Applicable requirements are identified from the selected standards and frameworks.

### 2. Requirement Mapping

Requirements are mapped to relevant Axiom policies, procedures, controls, processes, and security activities.

### 3. Evidence Review

Available evidence is reviewed to determine whether the requirement is adequately supported.

Example evidence may include:

* Policies
* Procedures
* Risk registers
* Statements of Applicability
* Control assessments
* Security assessments
* Incident records
* Vulnerability records
* Remediation records
* BIA and BCP documentation
* Disaster recovery documentation
* Tabletop exercise records
* Training records
* Management review records
* Security metrics
* Third-party assessments

### 4. Compliance Determination

Each requirement is assigned an assessment status based on the available evidence and implementation state.

### 5. Gap Identification

Where requirements are not fully satisfied, the assessment documents the relevant gap or deficiency.

### 6. Risk Evaluation

Significant compliance gaps are evaluated based on their potential security, operational, regulatory, contractual, or business impact.

### 7. Remediation Assignment

Identified gaps are assigned an owner, remediation action, priority, and target completion date.

### 8. Management Reporting

Assessment results are consolidated into an overall compliance posture for management review.

## Assessment Status Definitions

The following statuses are used:

| Status                  | Definition                                                                            |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Compliant**           | Requirement is implemented and supported by sufficient evidence.                      |
| **Partially Compliant** | Requirement is implemented in part, but one or more deficiencies remain.              |
| **Non-Compliant**       | Requirement is not adequately implemented or supported by evidence.                   |
| **Not Applicable**      | Requirement does not apply to the defined assessment scope.                           |
| **Under Review**        | Additional evidence or analysis is required before a final determination can be made. |

---

## Evidence Rating

Evidence quality is evaluated separately from compliance status.

| Rating       | Definition                                                                  |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Strong**   | Current, relevant, documented evidence clearly demonstrates implementation. |
| **Adequate** | Evidence generally supports implementation but may have minor limitations.  |
| **Limited**  | Evidence exists but is incomplete, outdated, or insufficiently detailed.    |
| **Missing**  | Required evidence was not available for assessment.                         |

Evidence gaps should not automatically be treated as control failures. However, insufficient evidence may prevent the organization from demonstrating that a requirement is effectively implemented.

---

## Compliance Risk Rating

Identified compliance gaps are evaluated using a risk-based approach.

| Rating       | Description                                                                                            |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Critical** | Significant exposure requiring immediate management attention.                                         |
| **High**     | Material security, operational, regulatory, or contractual exposure requiring prioritized remediation. |
| **Moderate** | Meaningful deficiency requiring planned remediation.                                                   |
| **Low**      | Limited exposure that can generally be addressed through routine improvement activities.               |

Risk ratings should consider:

* Business impact
* Security impact
* Likelihood
* Regulatory or contractual implications
* Customer impact
* Existing compensating controls
* Evidence quality
* Duration of the deficiency

## Compliance Findings

Identified deficiencies should be documented as formal findings.

Each finding should include:

1. Finding ID
2. Requirement reference
3. Description
4. Evidence
5. Compliance status
6. Risk rating
7. Business/security impact
8. Root cause or contributing factor
9. Recommended remediation
10. Responsible owner
11. Target date
12. Validation method
13. Final disposition

Findings should be written objectively and supported by available evidence.

## Governance and Escalation

Significant compliance issues should be escalated according to their risk and business impact.

Escalation may be appropriate when:

* A high or critical compliance gap is identified.
* A remediation deadline is missed.
* A control deficiency creates material exposure.
* Required evidence cannot be produced.
* A regulatory or contractual obligation may be affected.
* A risk owner cannot accept the residual risk.
* A remediation action requires executive resources or funding.

Management decisions should be documented and retained as governance evidence.
